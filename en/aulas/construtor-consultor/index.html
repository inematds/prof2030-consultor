Lesson 1 · 15 min

From builder to consultant

When everyone can build an agent, building turns into a price auction. The owner still doesn't know where to start, what is worth automating, where the risk is, or whether it worked. That is what the consultant delivers: clarity before the build and proof after it.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Why building got cheap and trust got expensive
  2. 2The four boxes of a diagnosis: person, agent, simple automation, ceases to exist
  3. 3What the consultant delivers and what they are not
  4. 4Write your positioning statement in two lines

Goal: explain why the diagnosis is worth more than the agent and write your own positioning statement as an AI consultant.

  1. 1/6

    The request

    The owner arrives with a tool in mind, almost always seen in a video or at a competitor. The request is the starting point of the conversation, not the specification of the work.

  2. 2/6

    Diagnosis

    Find the process that holds back the result, measure what it costs today (the baseline), and split the work into the four boxes: stays with the person, goes to an agent, stays simple automation, or ceases to exist.

  3. 3/6

    Priority

    From the opportunities found, pick one or two that deliver quick value and are feasible with the data and the team the company already has. Also say what NOT to do.

  4. 4/6

    Pilot

    Test it for 30 days, with one team, one metric, and a stop criterion agreed on beforehand. If it doesn't work, switch it off with no harm done.

  5. 5/6

    Measured value

    Compare against the baseline at 30, 60, and 90 days. This is where the consultant becomes a long-term partner: the model changes, the process changes, and someone needs to check that it's still worth it.

  6. 6/6

    The whole chain

    Request, diagnosis, priority, pilot, and measured value. Without the diagnosis, the agent solves the wrong problem; without measurement, no one knows whether it helped.

The builder comes in on the last step. The consultant answers for all five.

02 · What changes for people who work with AI

Four ideas that underpin the program.

1. Building got cheap

Models get cheaper and more capable every few months. In September 2026, Claude Opus 5.5 arrived costing 40% less than Opus 5 in typical use, according to Anthropic.

Anyone who only sells "I'll build your agent" is competing on price with a subscription the client can also sign up for.

ConsequenceThe value moves away from "knowing how to do it" and toward knowing what to do, where the risk is, and whether it worked.

2. Adoption is still rare

In a 2026 Gartner survey of technology executives, only 17% of organizations already had AI agents in use, and more than 60% expected to have them within two years.

Between interest and real use there is a gap: choosing the process, preparing data, testing small, and measuring.

Where you come inIn the gap between "we want AI" and "we know AI helped."

3. The four boxes

Every process you map comes out split into four: what stays with a person (judgment, relationships), what goes to an agent (a path that changes each time), what becomes simple automation (fixed rule), and what should stop existing.

The fourth box usually gives the cheapest win.

Example at HorizonteOverdue invoice reminder: simple automation. Answering a product question on WhatsApp: agent with limits. Approving a discount above the price list: person. A report nobody reads: stops existing.

4. What you are not

An AI consultant is not a security auditor, a lawyer, or a legacy-system programmer. When the case calls for a legal opinion, a penetration test, or heavy integration, you bring in someone qualified.

The INEMA certification in this program is a qualification, not a regulated profession. Tell the client this.

Ready-made line"I do the diagnosis, the prioritization, and the measurement. For formal LGPD (Brazil's data protection law) and security, I bring in a specialist."

03 · Templates: positioning and scope card

Copy it, replace what is between < >, and read it out loud.

Positioning statement (2 lines)
I help <type of company> <result as a number or decision>
starting with a <time frame> diagnosis.
I don't <what you don't do: sell tools before the diagnosis,
program legacy systems, give legal opinions>.
Scope card (5 lines)
I DO: discovery, diagnosis, prioritization, pilot plan, 30/60/90 measurement.
I DON'T: legal opinions, penetration tests, integration with legacy systems.
I CALL A SPECIALIST WHEN: <sensitive data at scale, contract, security>.
MY RESULT IS MEASURED BY: <client indicator against the baseline>.
MY CERTIFICATION: INEMA qualification (not a regulated profession).
Prompt: stress test of the statement
You are the owner of a distribution company with 40 employees, skeptical about AI.
Read my positioning statement below and:
1) say in one line what you understood I sell;
2) ask the 3 toughest questions you would ask before hiring me;
3) point out any jargon word or promise you don't believe.
Don't compliment me.

Statement: <paste here>
Simulation: illustrative example

I help distributors and wholesalers of up to 100 people to cut customer response time without growing the team, starting with a two-week diagnosis.

I don't sell tools before the diagnosis or program the company's management system.

  • Naming a tool ("WhatsApp agent specialist"): the tool changes every quarter; the client's problem stays.
  • Promising a number ("I'll raise your sales by 30%"): you haven't measured anything yet.
  • Empty second line: someone who doesn't say what they don't do looks like they do everything.

04 · Practice: your position

Two short exercises. Do them before moving on to lesson 2.

E1 · Positioning statement

15 min

Fill in the two-line template. Run the stress-test prompt and rewrite until it finds no jargon or promises.

Done when: the statement has two lines, names no tool, and the second line says what you don't do.

E2 · Scope card

10 min

Fill in the five-line card and show the statement and the card to two people who don't work with AI.

Done when: both people can repeat, in their own words, what you do and what you don't do.
