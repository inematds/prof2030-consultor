Lesson 6 · 18 min

Really measuring: 30, 60, and 90 days

"The agent answered 4,000 messages" doesn't tell you whether the company gained anything. Really measuring means comparing against the number from before, on the same scale, and also looking at what may have gotten worse. At 30, 60, and 90 days the question changes: does it work, is it stable, was it worth the money?

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The three indicators: result, operation, and safeguard
  2. 2Vanity metrics and the target that teaches people to cheat
  3. 3What you ask at 30, 60, and 90 days
  4. 4ROI and payback in owner's language

Goal: build a dashboard of 3 indicators with before-and-after values and run the 30-, 60-, and 90-day reviews against the baseline.

01 · The three-indicator dashboard

One of each type, always with the before value. Click each one.

Lab counterexample (F11): the Horizonte dashboard shows messages answered and conversations started, with no baseline column and no sales or complaints.

Weekly or monthly frequency, never daily: Monday has a backlog and Friday has people leaving early.

02 · The three reviews

Each review answers a different question.

1. 30 days

End of the pilot. Is the agent being used? Did operations improve against the baseline? Did any protection get worse?

Fix the usage friction: where the team gives up, where they go back to the old way. Decision: continue, adjust, or stop.

Warning signUsage dropping in the third week: the process didn't fit into the routine. Go back to the diagnosis.

2. 60 days

Did the gain hold without the consultant around? Does the real cost per task match what you calculated in lesson 4? Did errors go down, or just move somewhere else?

Compare the gain estimated at the start with the real one.

Question"If I disappear for a month, does this keep working?"

3. 90 days

Now the result indicator has had time to move. Calculate the ROI and the payback, and decide: expand to another team, keep it as it is, or turn it off.

What you measured becomes the baseline for the next cycle.

Owner's language"We invested R$ X, we earn R$ Y per month, the money comes back in Z months."

4. Watch out for the target

An indicator on its own becomes a target: response time drops because people answer anything; support closes tickets without solving them; discounts go up to close a sale.

That's why each operations indicator goes together with a protection one. And the dashboard shows adoption: how many people use it per week.

Right pairResponse time (operations) + quotes with the wrong price (protection).

ROI and payback
Monthly gain (R$)  = (value before - value after) x volume x unit cost
                     (e.g.: minutes saved x orders x cost per minute)
Monthly cost (R$)  = tool + tokens + human review + maintenance
Investment (R$)    = diagnosis + pilot + rollout (one time)

Yearly ROI (%)     = (monthly gain x 12 - monthly cost x 12 - investment)
                     / (monthly cost x 12 + investment) x 100
Payback (months)   = investment / (monthly gain - monthly cost)

Use the MEASURED gain (dashboard), not the estimated one. If monthly gain <= monthly cost, there is no payback: the project doesn't pay for itself.
Simulation: illustrative example
  • Operation: median time to quote from 3 h 40 min → 50 min.
  • Protection: quotes with a wrong price from 8% → 6%.
  • Result: orders closed from quotes from 31% → 35%.
  • Adoption: 5 of 6 salespeople using it every week.

Investment R$ 18k; measured gain R$ 9k/month; cost R$ 2k/month. Payback = 18 ÷ (9 − 2) ≈ 2.6 months. ROI for the year = (108 − 24 − 18) ÷ (24 + 18) ≈ 157%.

Illustrative numbers, for practicing the math.

  • Vanity metric: messages answered and conversations started measure motion, not results.
  • No "before" column: the period starts on the day the agent was turned on (this is failure F11 from the lab).
  • Changing the yardstick: measuring before by sample and after by the system gives numbers that can't be compared.
  • ROI with the estimated gain: on day 90, use the measured one.

04 · Practice: the Horizonte dashboard

15 min

Done when: you wrote, in up to 5 lines, why the current dashboard proves nothing.

E2 · A 3-metric dashboard

30 min

Build the 30/60/90 dashboard for the lesson 5 pilot: one result metric, one operation metric, and one protection metric, with how to measure it and the before value (real from the lab, or simulated, marked as a simulation).

Done when: the dashboard has 3 metrics with before and after values filled in, and a line saying who measures and where the number comes from.

E3 · ROI and payback

15 min

Using the numbers from your dashboard and an estimated investment, calculate ROI and payback, and write the sentence for Carlos in owner's language.

Done when: the sentence fits in two lines and has the amount invested, the monthly gain, and the payback period.
