Lesson 5 · 18 min

30-day pilot with an agent

A pilot is not a small version of the project: it is an experiment with a question, a deadline, and an exit rule. When the success criterion and the stop criterion are agreed on before you start, nobody argues later about whether it worked. And if the tool is discontinued along the way, the plan already says how to switch.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The question the pilot answers and the smallest possible experiment
  2. 2The four weeks: prepare, rehearse, run with approval, decide
  3. 3Success criterion, stop criterion, and who pulls the plug
  4. 4Exit plan for when the tool is discontinued

Goal: design a 30-day pilot on one page, with a metric, agent limits, a stop criterion, and an exit plan.

  1. 1/5

    Week 1

    Frozen baseline (the number from before), 5 to 10 real cases written with the owner, agent rules and limits approved, minimum permissions set up. Nothing talks to a customer yet.

  2. 2/5

    Week 2

    The agent runs on the test cases, internally. A person checks every output. If it gets wrong what it can't get wrong (price, discount, customer data), go back to week 1.

  3. 3/5

    Week 3

    With real customers, but every action that involves money goes through a person first. Everything is logged: what the agent proposed, who approved it, what changed.

  4. 4/5

    Week 4

    Compare against the baseline and answer: continue, adjust, or stop. Stopping is also a result: you saved the big project.

  5. 5/5

    Small, measured, and reversible

    Prepare, rehearse internally, run with approval, and decide against the baseline. If something serious happens, shut it off right away and go back to the manual process.

One team, one process, one main metric. A pilot that touches four areas teaches nothing.

02 · What can't be missing

Four pieces of the one-page plan.

1. The question

"Does an agent that drafts the quote reduce response time without increasing price errors?" One question only, with a number.

Cut the scope until it fits in 30 days: one product category, one salesperson, one channel. Manual integration is acceptable in the pilot.

Horizonte scopeOnly restock orders from existing customers, only on Patrícia's WhatsApp, for 30 days.

2. Agent limits

Write down what the agent can do, what it can do only with approval, and what it never does. Discounts with a cap, orders above a set amount with approval, no deleting, and nothing sent outside the workflow.

Every action is logged. This is what the Auditor will ask for later.

Lab reminderFailures F5 (discount with no cap) and F7 (no log and no approval above R$ 5 mil) are exactly what the plan must prevent.

3. Stop criterion

Stop right away if a safeguard fails: price error, discount above the cap, customer complaint, personal data exposed. Don't decide because "it's winning" in the first week: a small sample misleads.

The person who shuts it off has a name, not a job title, and a deadline to go back to manual (e.g., 2 hours).

Sample ruleWith fewer than 30 cases in the period, the result is "insufficient sample," not a win.

4. Exit plan

Agent tools get discontinued. OpenAI announced on 06/03/2026 that Agent Builder will be shut down on 11/30/2026, with migration to the Agents SDK or to ChatGPT Workspace Agents.

That's why the instructions, the test cases, and the data stay in the client's files, outside the tool.

Plan clause"If the tool is announced to be discontinued, we migrate within 15 days using the saved instructions and test cases."

03 · Template: one-page pilot plan

Fill it in with the owner before you start. Once signed, it becomes the contract for the experiment.

Pilot plan (1 page)
PILOT · <company> · <process> · <start date> to <end date (30 days)>

1. QUESTION: <does change X improve Y without worsening Z?>
2. SCOPE: <1 team>, <1 channel>, <1 type of case>. Outside the pilot: <...>
3. BASELINE (measured on <date>, by <who>, from <where the number comes from>):
   main metric: <value today>   goal: <value> in 30 days
   safeguards (cannot get worse): <price error>, <complaints>, <average discount>
4. TOOL AND VENDOR: <which>, monthly cost cap: R$ <...>
   data that goes in: <...>  data that does NOT go in: <CPF, ...>
5. AGENT LIMITS:
   can: <read orders, draft a reply>
   only with approval from <name>: <send a quote, order above R$ ...>
   never: <give a discount above X%, delete, send outside the workflow>
   log: every action with date, proposal, who approved
6. WEEKS: W1 prepare | W2 rehearse internally | W3 run with approval | W4 decide
7. SUCCESS CRITERION: <main metric reaches X with N >= 30 cases and no safeguard gets worse>
8. STOP CRITERION (right away): <price error>, <discount above the cap>, <complaint>, <data exposed>
   who shuts it off: <name>   back to manual in: <2 hours>
9. EXIT PLAN: instructions, test cases, and data stored in <client folder>;
   if the tool is announced to be discontinued, migrate in <15 days>.
10. DECISION ON DAY 30: [continue] [adjust and repeat] [stop]   signed by: <owner> and <consultant>
Simulation: illustrative example

Question: does the agent's quote draft reduce the time between the message and the quote sent, without increasing price errors?

Baseline: 50 requests measured by Patrícia Lima: median of 3 h 40 min; 4 quotes with the wrong price out of 50.

Goal: median under 1 hour, with price errors equal or lower.

Limits: maximum discount of 5%; quotes above R$ 5 mil only with Patrícia's approval; every action logged.

Stop: any quote sent with a discount above 5% or a price complaint. Who shuts it off: Rafael (IT), within 2 h.

  • No baseline: on day 30 it becomes opinion against opinion.
  • Big scope: four areas at once and no conclusion.
  • Stop criterion written after the problem: by then it's too late.
  • Everything inside the tool: if it gets discontinued, you start over from zero.

15 min

Ask an AI or a colleague to read the plan like a suspicious board member and list 5 ways the pilot could go wrong. Adjust the plan for each one.

Done when: each of the 5 risks has a line in the plan (limit, safeguard, or stop criterion) that deals with it.
