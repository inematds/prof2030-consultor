Lesson 7 · 18 min

Proposal, scope and price

Most fights with clients come from poorly written scope, not from bad work. A one-page proposal with today's number, what's in, what is NOT in, and the acceptance criterion protects both sides. The price comes from the method, never from improvising in the middle of a conversation.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The blocks of the one-page proposal
  2. 2Exclusions, client obligations, and acceptance criterion
  3. 3Three ways to charge: by the hour, by package, and by value
  4. 4Holding the scope when the client asks for "just one more thing"

Goal: write a one-page proposal with baseline, exclusions, client obligations, acceptance criterion, and package pricing.

01 · The offer ladder

Each step is a package with its own timeline, deliverable, and price. Click each one.

If you skip the diagnosis and sell the rollout directly, you take on the risk of building the wrong thing.

Each step is sold only after the previous one has delivered. The client can stop at any of them.

02 · How to charge and what to write

Four proposal decisions.

1. By the hour, by package, or by value

By the hour punishes efficiency: with AI you do in 4 hours what used to take 10, and you earn less. By package (fixed price per deliverable) is the default: estimated hours × your rate, plus a margin for the unexpected, checked against the value the client gains.

By value (a share of the gain) only works with a measured baseline and access to the number.

Price testIs the price well below the measured annual gain? If not, reduce the scope, not the quality.

2. What is NOT included

Write down what stays out: integration with the management system, API costs and licenses (passed through at cost), legal opinion, penetration testing, new use cases.

A new request in the middle of the project becomes a separate proposal, with its own price.

Ready-made line"Great idea. It goes in the queue for the next phase, with its own proposal, so it doesn't delay this one."

3. Client obligations

Access to the data and screens, one person with decision-making power available a few hours a week, validations on the agreed schedule, and a sponsor on the board.

If the client doesn't meet them, the deadline moves too. It's written down.

At HorizontePatrícia Lima measures the baseline; Rafael grants the access; Carlos Mendes approves the phases.

4. Acceptance criterion

The acceptance criterion says when the work is delivered: objective, yes or no, agreed beforehand. "Report delivered with all 6 sections and presented to the board" is acceptance. "Client satisfied" is not.

Never promise the business result ("+30% in sales"): promise the method, the measurement, and the deliverable.

Pilot acceptance"Pilot run for 30 days, dashboard with before and after, and decision recorded."

03 · Template: one-page proposal

Fill it in after discovery. The values between < > are yours: there is no market price here.

Proposal (1 page)
PROPOSAL · <company> · <date> · valid until <date>

1. WHAT WE HEARD: <the pain in the client's own words, 2 lines>
2. BASELINE: <metric> = <value> (measured on <date>, by <who>, <how>)
   Goal: <metric> from <value> to <value> in <timeframe>, without worsening <safeguard>
3. WHAT WE WILL DELIVER (package <Diagnosis | Pilot | Wave N | Follow-up>):
   - <deliverable 1>  - <deliverable 2>  - <deliverable 3>
4. WHAT IS NOT INCLUDED:
   - integration with <system>   - API/license costs (paid by the client, at cost)
   - legal opinion and security testing (we refer you to a specialist)
   - new use cases (separate proposal)
5. CLIENT OBLIGATIONS: access to <data/screens> by <date>; <name> available <N> h/week;
   approvals within <N> business days; sponsor: <name>
6. TIMELINE AND PHASES: <week 1-2: ...> <week 3-4: ...>
7. ACCEPTANCE CRITERION: <objective, yes/no>
8. RISKS: <what might not work> | what the company gives up: <...>
9. INVESTMENT: R$ <value> (<package, fixed price>), payment <e.g.: 50% at start, 50% at acceptance>
10. NEXT STEP: <client task> by <date>; next conversation: <date>
Calculating the package price
Estimated hours per deliverable ............ <h>
Your hourly rate (floor) ................ R$ <rate>
Package floor = h x rate ............... R$ <...>
+ contingency margin (rule of thumb 15-20%) R$ <...>
= Proposed price ........................ R$ <...>
Check: price < client's measured/estimated annual gain? [yes/no]
If not: reduce scope (fewer deliverables), never quality.
