AI Consultant: from diagnosis to measured value

Building with AI has become cheap. The hard part is choosing the right process, testing small, and proving it was worth it. In eight lessons you learn to help owners and the board adopt AI with diagnosis, priorities, a pilot, and measurement.

Start with lesson 1 See Professional 2030
  • 8lessons of 15 to 20 min
  • 6templates to copy
  • 1company to diagnose
  • 90measurement days in the plan

The lessons

Each lesson has a diagram, a template to copy, a practice with a "done when" and a commented quiz. The practice is always in the same company: Horizonte Distribuidora.

  1. Lesson 1 · 15 minFrom builder to consultantWhen everyone can build an agent, building turns into a price auction. The owner still doesn't know where to start, what is worth automating, where the risk is, or whether it worked. That is what the consultant delivers: clarity before the build and proof after it.
  2. Lesson 2 · 20 minThe discovery conversationNobody leaves the doctor's office with a prescription before the exam. In AI consulting, this happens all the time: the owner describes a pain and the consultant already answers with a tool. In 45 minutes of a well-run conversation, you walk out with the bottleneck, a number for today, and the list of what's already running in the shadows of the company.
  3. Lesson 3 · 18 minValue × feasibility (and when NOT to use AI)Discovery always brings more ideas than the company can take on. If you don't choose, the owner picks the flashiest one. The value and feasibility matrix puts the opportunities side by side, and the "when not to use AI" test takes off the list whatever a simple rule solves better.
  4. Lesson 4 · 20 minModel and vendor by cost per taskThe owner asks: "which AI should I use?" The answer is not the trendy model or the cheapest per token. It is the one that delivers the task at the lowest total cost, counting the retries and the time of whoever reviews. And the vendor weighs in too: tools get discontinued, data crosses borders, licenses change.
  5. Lesson 5 · 18 min30-day pilot with an agentA pilot is not a small version of the project: it is an experiment with a question, a deadline, and an exit rule. When the success criterion and the stop criterion are agreed on before you start, nobody argues later about whether it worked. And if the tool is discontinued along the way, the plan already says how to switch.
  6. Lesson 6 · 18 minReally measuring: 30, 60, and 90 days"The agent answered 4,000 messages" doesn't tell you whether the company gained anything. Really measuring means comparing against the number from before, on the same scale, and also looking at what may have gotten worse. At 30, 60, and 90 days the question changes: does it work, is it stable, was it worth the money?
  7. Lesson 7 · 18 minProposal, scope and priceMost fights with clients come from poorly written scope, not from bad work. A one-page proposal with today's number, what's in, what is NOT in, and the acceptance criterion protects both sides. The price comes from the method, never from improvising in the middle of a conversation.
  8. Lesson 8 · 20 minFinal project: the Horizonte diagnosis + What changedNow you put it all together: the complete diagnosis and adoption plan for Horizonte Distribuidora, in one page for Carlos and appendices for anyone who wants to check. The rubric says what needs to be there. At the end, the dashboard of what changed in the market this quarter, so your account doesn't go stale.

Who it's for

  1. 1People who want to help owners and the board adopt AI with a method, without selling a tool.
  2. 2Professionals in processes, management, IT, or consultative sales. No programming needed.
  3. 3People who already build with AI and want to move on to diagnosing, prioritizing, and measuring.
  4. 4Recommended first: the Common Core (not required).

What you will be able to do

  1. 1Lead a 45-minute discovery conversation and leave with the baseline.
  2. 2Prioritize opportunities by value and feasibility, including when NOT to use AI.
  3. 3Compare models and vendors by the cost per task, with human review included.
  4. 4Design a 30-day pilot with a stop criterion, measure at 30/60/90 days, and write the one-page proposal.
